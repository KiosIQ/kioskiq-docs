    02

    Read an order ticket

    Every ticket carries the same six things in the same places. Once you know where to look, you can read one at a glance across the kitchen.

    Anyone
    On the ticketWhat it tells you
    Coloured bandThe status, in colour so you can read it from a distance. Amber pending or preparing, blue confirmed, green ready.
    Payment badgeCASH RECEIVED means paid. UNPAID means the counter still has to take money — cook it, but do not hand it over without checking.
    Order numberWhat the counter will call out. This is the number the guest knows.
    CustomerA name, a table, or Walk-in Customer.
    Item linesWhat to make, with quantities. A long list is cut short on the card — the full list is on the printed ticket.
    Time of OrderWhen it came in. Your oldest ticket is always furthest left.
    ETAWhen it is promised. It turns red and counts up as overdue by N min once that time passes.
    Grab & Go / For HereWhether it is being taken away or eaten in. Changes how you plate it.
    Cutlery icon and numberHow many covers, so you send out the right number of sets.
    🖨Reprints that ticket — see task 07.
    The ETA is a promise, not a guess It is what the guest was told at the counter or on their phone. If a ticket is drifting towards overdue and you know it will not make it, tell the counter now, while they can still say something to the guest. A ticket that goes red silently is a complaint waiting to happen.
    03

    Move an order through the kitchen

    Four taps from arrival to handover. The button on the ticket is always the next one — you cannot get the order wrong.

    Anyone
    1. Mark Confirmed — you have seen it and you are taking it. The counter can see it has been accepted.
    2. Mark Preparing — it is on. The band turns amber and the ETA restarts from now, so the clock you are being judged against begins when you press this, not when the order arrived.
    3. Mark Ready — it is plated or bagged. The band turns green, the ETA is replaced by Ready at, and the counter knows to call the guest.
    4. Mark Completed — it has gone to the guest. The ticket leaves the board.
    5. Watch open in the bottom corner. That is your real workload, whatever the filters say.
    Press Preparing when you actually start Not when you read the ticket. Pressing it early starts the clock while the food is still sitting there, and makes every order look slower than it was. Pressing it honestly is what makes the ETA worth anything.
    There is no undo Tapping a status is one-way on this screen. Mark something Completed by mistake and it drops off the board — tell the counter straight away, because they can still find it in Order history. Tap deliberately; the buttons are big and close together.

    04

    See only what matters

    Six buttons across the top, each with a live count. Tap one to narrow the board to those tickets, tap it again to go back to everything.

    Anyone
    FilterShowsUse it when
    PENDINGJust arrived, nobody has accepted them.Start of a rush, to accept everything quickly.
    CONFIRMEDAccepted, not started.Deciding what to put on next.
    PREPARINGOn the pass right now.Checking what is actually cooking.
    READYMade, waiting to be handed over.Food is sitting and going cold — chase the counter.
    UNPAIDMoney has not been taken yet.Before anything leaves the kitchen. Never hand an unpaid order over without telling the counter.
    OVERDUEPast its promised time.Every few minutes. This is the one that costs you complaints.
    A habit worth having When the board looks overwhelming, tap OVERDUE first, clear that, then PREPARING. Working the oldest problem first is almost always right, and the counts tell you when you are winning.
    05

    Quick service mode

    For counters where food is handed over in a minute and four taps is three too many.

    Anyone
    1. Tap ⚡ Quick service mode in the top bar. It lights up when it's on.
    2. Every ticket now shows one green Mark Completed button, whatever its status.
    3. The PREPARING filter disappears, because nothing sits in that stage any more.
    4. Tap the button again to switch back. Nothing is lost either way.
    What you give up One tap means no Preparing and no Ready, so the counter cannot see that food is plated and waiting, and your ETA and timing figures stop meaning much. Good for a coffee bar. Poor for a kitchen where the counter relies on knowing when to call the guest.

    06

    Mark a dish sold out

    You know first when something runs out. Switch it off here and it stops being ordered — on the till, the kiosk and online — within seconds.

    Anyone
    1. Open the menu (top left) and choose Menu Items.
    2. Find the dish — search, pick a category, or filter by All / Available / Unavailable.
    3. Tap the badge in the corner of the card. Green Available becomes red Unavailable.Tap the badge, not the card. Tapping the middle of the card does nothing.
    4. Tap it again when the dish is back on.
    This is the fastest fix in the building The counter cannot see your walk-in. Turning a dish off from the kitchen stops the next twenty guests ordering it, before anyone has to apologise. It beats shouting through the hatch.
    Nothing turns back on by itself A dish stays off overnight and into tomorrow. Check the Unavailable filter at the start of every shift, or you will spend the day refusing food you have.

    07

    Reprint a ticket

    Paper ticket lost, torn, or covered in something? Print it again from the card.

    Anyone
    1. Find the order on the board.
    2. Tap the printer icon at the top right of that ticket.
    3. Check the paper came out. Reprinting does not change the order or its status.
    "Printer not configured" The printer button in the top bar shows this terminal's printer status. If it says not configured, nothing will come out of any reprint — the screen is working but no printer is attached to it. Tell your manager; it is a one-off setup, not something to work around all service.
    08

    Find one order

    When the counter asks "where's 47?", you need it in seconds, not a scroll.

    Anyone
    1. Tap the magnifier in the top bar. A Search orders box opens along the bar.
    2. Type the order number or the customer name. The board narrows as you type.
    3. Tap the ✕ to clear the search and get the whole board back.
    Long board? Jump to start and Jump to end at the bottom of the screen skip you to the oldest or newest ticket without dragging. Oldest is always on the left.
    09

    Tables and Rooms

    Two more screens in the menu, for sites that serve to a table or up to a hotel room.

    Anyone
    ScreenWhat it shows
    TablesEvery table with its seat count, filtered by state: Ready, Seated, Ordered, Served, Bill printed, To clean, Reserved. Useful from the pass for seeing which tables are waiting on food.
    RoomsRoom-service orders, grouped by wing and floor, filtered by Ready, Ordered, Served. Each tile is a room number and its code.
    Room service takes longer than you think A tray still has to travel. If your site runs rooms, agree with the counter what the ETA means — food ready at the pass, or food at the door — because the guest is timing the second one.
    10

    Look up a past order

    Completed tickets leave the board. They are not gone.

    Anyone
    1. Open the menu (top left).
    2. Choose Order History.
    3. Find the order to see what was on it.
    When to reach for it A guest comes back saying something was missing, or you completed a ticket before it went out. Check here first, then tell the counter what you found — refunds are theirs to do, not yours.
    11

    Sound, connection and refresh

    Three small icons that tell you whether to trust what is on the screen.

    Anyone
    IconMeansWhat to do
    🔔 BellSound on or off when a new order lands.Leave it on. In a loud kitchen it is the only thing that will get your attention.
    ≋ ConnectionGreen: online, receiving live orders. Anything else means new orders may not be reaching this screen.Check it whenever the board goes oddly quiet. A quiet Friday is usually a broken connection, not a quiet Friday.
    ⟳ RefreshPulls the latest orders now.If something looks stale or the counter says an order exists that you cannot see.
    Last updatedBottom right — the time the board last heard from the server.If that time is minutes old, the board is stale. Refresh, then check the connection icon.
    The dangerous failure is a silent one A frozen board looks exactly like a calm board. If you have not seen a new ticket in a while during service, do not assume it is quiet — glance at Last updated and the connection icon before you believe it.
    12

    Awkward situations

    What happens on a real shift, and what to do when the screen has no button for it.

    Anyone
    What's happeningWhat to do
    You tapped the wrong statusThere's no undo here. Tell the counter immediately — the order still exists and they can see it. Do not try to fix it by tapping other buttons.
    A ticket says UNPAIDMake it, but do not hand it over. The counter takes payment. If it leaves the kitchen unpaid, it lands on whoever was on shift.
    An order is going to be very lateTell the counter while there is still time to warn the guest. The board turning red on its own tells nobody anything.
    You've run out mid-serviceSwitch the dish off — task 06. Takes ten seconds and stops the next twenty orders.
    Guest asks about allergensThe ticket does not carry allergen information. Never guess. Send the question to the counter and check the allergen folder.
    Board hasn't moved in a whileCheck Last updated and the connection icon, then hit refresh. Do not assume it is quiet.
    Nothing printsCheck the printer status in the top bar. If it says not configured, the screen is fine but no printer is attached — tell your manager.
    Ticket is cut shortLong orders are trimmed on the card. Reprint it (task 07) to see every line.
